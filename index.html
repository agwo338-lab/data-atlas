          The pipeline

          Four layers, and the distinction between them matters more than the flow does. A trigger starts a run. A worker researches and reports — and cannot write a file, ever, because it holds no tool that could. The applier is the main session, the one thing here with write and git access, and it routes findings by confidence. The data files feed the page. data/sources.js is drawn apart on purpose: it is a ruleset that both the researcher and the page read, not a stage anything passes through.

          Why classes, not tiers

          The atlas used to call a claim confident when two reputable sources agreed. That test quietly failed on the most common case in this industry: an operator issues a press release, two trade outlets write it up, and the claim collects two ticks without anything ever having checked it. Both outlets are downstream of the same interested party. Independence has to be measured by who produced the evidence and why — which is what these classes encode.

          Where evidence comes from

          The working catalogue of channels. Regulatory and network sources are the ones worth the effort: they are filed or observed independently of any announcement, and most of them are diffable on a schedule rather than read once.

          The confidence rules

          • HighTwo or more distinct classes agree, at least one of them independent (regulatory, network, or observed), and the freshest evidence is inside this field's re-check window. This is the only level that gets applied and pushed without asking.
          • MediumTwo or more classes agree but all of them are the operator or outlets restating the operator — or a single regulatory record standing alone. Believable, uncorroborated.
          • LowOne class only, or every source is past its window, or sources genuinely conflict. Conflicts are never silently resolved.
          • UnverifiedNothing usable. Directory and unattributed sources are still cited on the entry, but they cannot lift a claim to any level above this.
